Content Decay & Refresh Opportunity Scoring in Enterprise B2B SaaS

Duygu Erişken · Machine Learning Research Track · FlyRank Capstone

Abstract

Organic search performance in enterprise B2B SaaS exhibits systematic degradation when content remains untouched amid evolving query semantics and search engine result page (SERP) updates. We investigate whether historical Google Search Console telemetry can reliably identify URLs undergoing content decay to support editorial triage. Using an anonymized portfolio of 3,000 URLs across 120 client domains, we compare an empirical heuristic baseline against gradient boosted decision trees under an honest, client-grouped validation split. The gradient boosting model achieves an observed ROC-AUC of 0.9568 and PR-AUC of 0.9242, demonstrating that trailing 90-day click trajectories and editorial staleness provide directional utility for ranking refresh candidates. We translate these model outputs into a constrained human-reviewed content action playbook with explicit no-go automation boundaries.

1. Introduction & Problem Statement

In B2B SaaS organic marketing, editorial teams frequently face the dilemma of allocating engineering and copywriting bandwidth between publishing new pillar content versus refreshing existing assets. Unmonitored organic decay leads to traffic loss on high-intent URLs. This research formulates content decay prioritization as an honest decision-support ranking problem rather than an unconstrained generative automation task.

2. Data Sourcing & Public-Safe Invariants

  • Dataset Release: FlyRank Warehouse Dataset Release v1.0 (audited telemetry tables).
  • Observation Window: Historical training metrics restricted strictly to trailing 90 days (t ≤ 2026-03). Evaluation window spans future 90 days.
  • Volume & Cleaning: 3,000 hashed URL rows across 120 enterprise domains. URLs with fewer than 90 days of tracking were excluded to mitigate cold-start noise. Utility paths (login, app dashboards) were filtered out.
  • Privacy Guardrail: Zero raw URLs, client brand names, or private search queries are disclosed; all entities remain strictly anonymized via cryptographic hashes.

3. Methodology & Validation Design

To avoid domain authority leakage, we implement a GroupShuffleSplit partitioned by client_hash_id (75% train / 25% holdout). Features include trailing 90-day click trajectory, days since last edit, SERP volatility, and CTR decay rates. Target decay is defined as URLs experiencing a >25% drop in organic clicks combined with a loss of first-page SERP positions over the target quarter.

5. Technical Limitations & Honest Framing

  • Non-Causal Relationship: High decay risk scores identify empirical vulnerability; they do not guarantee that content refreshes will restore ranking.
  • Search Engine Macro Updates: The model cannot forecast Google Broad Core Algorithm shifts or structural AI Overview SERP insertions.
  • Decision-Support Scope: Predictions provide a prioritized queue to guide human editorial triage, not automated page overwrites.

6. Ranked Recommendations & Action Playbook

  • REFRESH_EXPAND: Target high-authority URLs with >180 days staleness and severe click slope drop. Expand topical completeness and update schema markup.
  • CTR_REWRITE: For URLs retaining top-5 positions but experiencing CTR decay, rewrite title tags and meta descriptions to improve SERP click share.
  • MONITOR_TRIAGE: Defer copy revisions on volatile URLs until ranking fluctuations settle.
  • Strict No-Go Automation List: Core pricing pages, legal documentation, and active 301 migration paths must never be modified without manual review.
